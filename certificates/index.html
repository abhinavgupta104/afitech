What it actually means

A certificate is only worth what it changes

Plenty of suppliers display a logo. These are the three things certification actually obliges us to do, and the ones worth asking any supplier about.

  • The process is documented, not remembered

    Every stage from drawing review to final inspection runs to a written procedure. The person who printed your last order does not have to be the person who prints the next one for it to come out the same.

  • Problems are traced, not just fixed

    Non-conformities are recorded with a root cause and a corrective action. If a panel fails inspection, the reason is logged and the process changed — not just the part remade.

  • It is audited by someone outside the company

    Certification is not self-declared. Surveillance audits are scheduled for 16 November 2026 and 16 November 2027, and the certificate lapses if they are not passed.
