Chapter one01

You used one today and never noticed it.

A keypad is the only component of a machine that every single operator puts a finger on, every shift, for years. It is also the only one nobody is ever asked to think about.

That is the job. A front panel has done its work when it disappears — when the operator is watching the process, not the panel, and the key answers anyway.

When it is right, nobody mentions it.

Chapter two02

Six sheets, about a millimetre thick.

A membrane keypad looks like a printed sticker. It is a laminated assembly, and every sheet in it is doing exactly one job.

  1. 01

    Graphic overlay

    Polyester, 0.175–0.25 mm

    The face. Your legends and branding, printed on its underside and protected by the film itself.

  2. 02

    Overlay adhesive

    3M 467MP / 468MP acrylic

    Bonds the face to the circuit, and forms the perimeter seal that keeps fluid out of the stack.

  3. 03

    Upper circuit

    Silver conductive ink on polyester

    The moving half of every switch. Screen-printed traces that come down to meet the pads below.

  4. 04

    Spacer

    Die-cut polyester, adhesive both faces

    Holds the two circuits apart. The hole under each key is the switch — its size sets the feel.

  5. 05

    Lower circuit

    Silver ink, tail routed to your connector

    The fixed half, and the tail that carries every key back to your board.

  6. 06

    Rear adhesive

    Die-cut to your panel outline

    What holds the finished assembly onto the equipment, cut to the outline on your drawing.

Laminated in register, the whole assembly is between 0.6 mm and 1.6 mm thick — thinner than the panel it is stuck to.

Chapter three03

Why the legend never wears off.

Find a machine that has been in service twenty years. The paint has gone chalky. The labels on the casing have curled at the corners. The keypad legend is perfect.

That is not luck, and it is not better ink. It is where the ink is.

The print is on the back of the film.

Every overlay we produce is screen-printed in reverse, on the underside of the polyester, so the ink ends up sealed between the film and the adhesive. Nothing a finger, a cloth or a solvent touches ever reaches it. To scratch the legend away you would have to destroy the window above it first.

Chapter six06

Where they end up.

Nine sectors, each asking something slightly different of the same six sheets of polyester.

Non-tactile keypads

2 sectors

  • Medical equipment

    DemandPanels are wiped down between patients, often many times a day, with alcohol and disinfectants that destroy printed labels and attack unsealed seams.

    AnswerSub-surface printing puts the legend under the film where cleaning agents cannot reach it, and a continuous perimeter seal keeps fluid out of the stack entirely.

  • Access control & security

    DemandKeypads are mounted outdoors or in unsupervised locations, used constantly, and must resist both weather and deliberate abuse.

    AnswerA sealed non-tactile stack with no moving parts has nothing to force and nothing to wear, and takes five million actuations.

Tactile keypads

2 sectors

  • Industrial control

    DemandOperators key while watching a process rather than the panel, frequently wearing gloves, in environments with dust, oil and vibration.

    AnswerA stainless dome gives a snap that is felt through a glove, in a stack that is sealed against the surrounding environment.

  • Automation

    DemandMachine HMIs are operated thousands of times per shift and cannot be taken out of service for a keypad failure.

    AnswerDomes soften gradually rather than failing suddenly, so a panel signals its own end of life well before it stops working.

Graphic overlays

2 sectors

  • Testing & measuring equipment

    DemandInstrument fronts carry dense legends, fine symbols and display windows, and are expected to stay legible for the working life of the instrument.

    AnswerScreen-printed overlays hold fine detail, and tinted or anti-glare windows raise display contrast under bench lighting.

  • Aviation & aerospace

    DemandPanels must be readable in direct sunlight and in darkness, and hold their finish and legends over long service intervals.

    AnswerAnti-glare hardcoats for sunlight, backlighting for night operation, and sub-surface legends that cannot wear off.

PCB-backed keypads

2 sectors

  • Automotive electronics

    DemandAssemblies face wide temperature swings, constant vibration, and must be compact enough for a dashboard or console.

    AnswerA rigid PCB-backed assembly survives vibration better than a flexible stack, and the circuit is rated from −35 °C to +90 °C.

  • Telecommunications

    DemandRack and cabinet equipment needs compact, low-profile interfaces with clear status indication in poorly lit rooms.

    AnswerEmbedded LEDs with a 180° viewing angle, read through dead-front windows that stay blank until lit.

Embossed keypads

1 sector

  • Vending & kiosks

    DemandPublic-facing fascias take heavy, careless use and carry branding that has to survive it.

    AnswerEmbossed keys locate the finger without looking, and hardcoated polyester resists scratching and repeated cleaning.
